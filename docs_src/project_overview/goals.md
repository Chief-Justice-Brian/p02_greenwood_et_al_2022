# Goals

## Objectives

- Reproduce the assigned exhibits of Greenwood, Hanson, Shleifer & Sørensen
  (2022), "Predictable Financial Crises" (Tables 1, 3, 4 and Figures 1, 3),
  entirely from free public data sources. No official replication package
  exists; this is, to our knowledge, the first public end-to-end
  reconstruction of the paper.
- Reproduce every exhibit a second time with data through the present,
  keeping the historical R-zone thresholds frozen so post-publication data
  never moves the historical classifications. The country-by-year R-zone
  tracker is the product form of this requirement.
- Extend the crisis chronology past 2016 by re-applying BVX's own published
  criteria (a 30%+ bank equity decline plus narrative evidence of widespread
  failures or panic) to new public data, so "crisis" means the same thing in
  every year of the panel.
- Examine the post-publication period: whether the warning signal was on in
  2020–2022 before the March 2023 US banking stress, and how the dynamic
  specifications the paper asserts in footnote 10 compare against its static
  baseline.
- Automate everything: one `doit` invocation pulls, cleans, analyzes, and
  compiles the report, with the cleaning stage separated from the analysis
  and every reported statistic generated by code.

## Success Criteria

- A fresh clone builds end to end with `conda env create`, `conda activate`,
  and `doit`, with no API keys or credentials.
- The full `pytest` suite passes, with paper-benchmark differences that exceed
  the paper-scaled policy retained as strict expected failures in
  `src/test_paper_exhibits.py`.
- Every assigned exhibit exists in both its historical-replication and
  post-publication form, and the R-zone tracker classifies every sample
  country through the present.
- The final LaTeX report compiles with no hand-typed numbers and no code
  listings, and the project-tour notebook executes cleanly from the built
  data.
